# DFL decision log

The authority for every rule is the living doc **"DFL Rulebook: The Synthesis"** (https://claude.ai/code/artifact/0e15335e-d40e-4e7a-8d2a-e9eccedb24ea), frozen as v1 on 2026-10-06. It decides each rule by one test, in order: the Commissioner's skeleton, then the NFL rule, then the NFL rule adapted to 48 teams / 18 games / exile, then the fair-competitiveness bands. Its machine-readable form is `engine/rulebook.py`; `engine/check_rulebook.py` fails when the code and a rulebook row disagree, and writes `reports/rulebook_status.md` (every rule, its basis, and whether the code matches).

There is no "assumed" tag any more. Every rule has one of four bases (skeleton, nfl, adapted, fair); `engine/rules.py` carries the same labels, and simulation stand-ins that are not league rules are labelled `model`. The old decisions are archived in `docs/decisions_pre_rebase.md` (reference only).

The Commissioner delegated the open rulebook decisions to the AI on 2026-10-06 ("you have the authority"), to be decided by the NFL rule first and then by fair competitiveness. The alternatives for each are listed at the end of the Rulebook, so any row can be reopened.

To change a rule: change the number in `engine/rules.py`, update its row in `engine/rulebook.py`, and run `engine/check_rulebook.py`.

## Step 6a: earned-service foundation (2026-10-07)

Current decision order, authorized by the Commissioner: first ask what the NFL would likely do and why, adapted to DFL's schedule and structure; then test fair competitiveness; refer unresolved design choices to the Commissioner-elect. Existing DFL skeleton rules remain fixed.

The NFL distinguishes accrued seasons (six regular-season games on qualifying full-pay status, for free agency) from credited seasons (three, for minimum pay). Source: https://operations.nfl.com/calendar-events/nfl-free-agency/types-of-free-agents (checked 2026-10-07). Keeping the separate clocks prevents unsigned or practice-squad time from manufacturing free-agent rights or salary service.

`service.py` records qualifying games for the 53-player active/inactive roster and injured reserve, awards service once per season, and classifies expiring contracts as ERFA/RFA/UFA. Ambassador round-robin games count under the existing DFL rulebook. Byes, the Ambassador Bowl and playoffs do not count toward this regular-season service model. Practice-squad players earn service only after permanent promotion to the roster. Mid-season releases retain service earned before release; it is cumulative across teams, with a week counted only once. Counters and partial-year weeks persist through the existing player-body JSON save.

Historical limitation: founding roster players and old saves have no game-by-game service history, so their prior calendar-years estimate is retained once. Founding practice-squad players, drafted rookies and undrafted rookies start at zero. Future seasons are earned. NFI/PUP, suspensions, holdouts and injury settlements are not yet modelled and are not silently counted.

This is deliberately an accounting checkpoint: minimum salaries and practice-squad eligibility still use the old calendar-years field, and the re-signing policy is unchanged. The classifier does not enforce retention rights. Tenders, offer sheets, matching rights, tags, waivers and trade/pick ownership must be integrated before Step 6 can be called complete. Thus `fa.classes` has matching constants but a **partial** mechanism. The NFL itself makes ERFA rights conditional on offering a minimum one-year tender; classification alone cannot restrict a player.

## Step 6b: service controls eligibility and minimum base pay

Credited seasons now control market-price floors, founding contracts, offseason re-signings and signings, street replacements, promotions and continuing-contract minimum base pay. Accrued seasons now control the existing DFL practice-squad eligibility rule. Calendar years still govern age/career history; unsigned and practice-squad years no longer create salary or eligibility service. Newly generated street players have zero documented earned service rather than inferring it from age. Existing founding and old-save estimates remain unchanged.

The NFL minimum is base salary, not base plus bonus. A new contract caps its bonus allocation at the amount left after the credited-service base floor. This preserves the DFL rookie scale as an initial cap number; for a $0.30M late-round rookie, $0.29M is base and only $0.01M can be bonus proration. Continuing deals are raised when the earned minimum rises, without rewriting existing bonus proration or guaranteed dollars. A four-year low rookie deal therefore need not remain a flat cap number. The usual offseason cap/cutdown checks run after these increases. No pay-scale or fairness dial was changed.

Sources: NFL Football Operations, types of free agents and credited seasons (https://operations.nfl.com/calendar-events/nfl-free-agency/types-of-free-agents); contract language and minimum base salary (https://operations.nfl.com/calendar-events/nfl-free-agency/contract-language); NFL/NFLPA CBA Article 26 (https://nflpaweb.blob.core.windows.net/website/PDFs/CBA/March-15-2020-NFL-NFLPA-Collective-Bargaining-Agreement-Final-Executed-Copy.pdf).

Existing DFL simplification: the frozen practice-squad rule permits players with no more than two accrued seasons without the NFL's combined ten-player category limit and allows at most six veterans. Actual NFL eligibility has additional active-game categories and a combined quota. This increment corrects the service clock within the frozen DFL allocation; it does not claim exact NFL practice-squad implementation. Elevations remain unmodeled.

Step 6 is still incomplete: ERFA/RFA tenders, offer sheets, matching and compensation, tags, waivers, trades and draft-pick ownership/trading/compensatory picks remain. Re-signing still uses a probability policy rather than a contract table.

Validation complete: all twenty check scripts passed on the corrected Step 6b code, including six service and four salary/eligibility test cases. Both-engine fairness studies passed all thirteen bands, and the four-league cap study was refreshed. Interim pre-CBA results are superseded. Three forty-season hashes were regenerated for explained rule changes; no model dial or fairness band changed. See reports/verification_step6b.md and its JSON receipt for exact scope and log hashes.

Primary-source correction: NFL/NFLPA CBA Article 26 Section 2 explicitly excludes IR from salary-credited service, unlike accrued service for free agency. The NFL summary page did not state that exclusion. Step 6b therefore persists separate credited-service weeks; active/inactive roster games qualify for both clocks, IR games qualify only for accrued service. The same DFL Ambassador-round-robin inclusion applies to both clocks. Practice-squad, bye and postseason time earns neither. New boundary tests cover two roster games plus four IR games (accrued season only) and three roster games plus three IR games (both seasons).

Legacy migration limitation: old saves lack the roster/IR distinction. Closed historical service counters remain one-time estimates. For an old partial-year save missing credited-service weeks, its existing qualifying weeks are copied once as an estimate; subsequent seasons reset both lists and use the correct distinction. No past IR history is invented or silently reconstructed.


Regression-test corrections: the initial full run on corrected Step 6b engine code passed 18 scripts and failed two legacy assertions. The coach-centering test inspected a cached pre-turnover reference; it now explicitly refreshes the current staff reference at the next-season boundary. The media test measured a noisy final-year credibility snapshot (correlation 0.03); it now measures mean scored forecasts over careers with the same 0.05 threshold and separately checks that a better forecast earns more credibility against the same result and starting score. These are test-only changes; simulation code, model dials and fairness bands are unchanged. Both corrected scripts passed on rerun; the media career-score correlation is reported in the check log.

## Step 6c/6d (rebuilt): pick ownership, tenders, funded offers, matching and compensation

This increment was first built and verified in an earlier environment, but that work never reached GitHub or Drive and could not be recovered. It was rebuilt from the Drive documents on a new branch (`step6-rebuild`) from the published Step 6b checkpoint (61d49d6). The design below follows those documents; where they were silent the rebuild made the choices listed here.

**Pick ownership.** `engine/movement.py` keeps a ledger of ordinary picks for the coming draft and the three after it. Each pick separates its original club (which sets the slot), its current owner (who selects), its resolved slot, an offer reservation and the selection. The draft follows ownership; slot and rookie price belong to the slot. `transfer_pick` is the single door for moving a pick (offers use it now, trades will). Picks stay in the ledger for one year after their draft.

**Tenders.** An expiring player's class comes from `service.expiry_class`: exclusive rights below three accrued seasons, restricted at exactly three, unrestricted at four or more, restricted for every expiring player of an exiled club. A tender creates rights and a one-year contract at the tender price (first 2.69, second 1.93, original 1.22, refusal 1.18; never below 110% of prior base; exclusive rights at the minimum rung). Practice-squad wages are for the year and carry no rights (NFL). UFAs cannot be tendered.

**Offers.** Another club may make a binding offer to a restricted free agent while her tender window is open. It must be funded (cap room is held back while the offer is open), at least the tender, and carry compensation: a pick the offering club owns in the tender round or an earlier one (own-or-better, exact round first), held back too. First-round and second-round tenders ask for those rounds; original-round asks for the round she was drafted; right of first refusal asks for nothing. The prior club has five days to match every represented term (salary, years, bonus share, guarantee years) and needs the cap room for the difference. A match releases both holds. If it does not match, the player and the compensation move together or not at all; every check happens before any change. Competing offers for one player, NFL incentives and special clauses, and unsigned-rights carryover are not supported.

**Storage.** The ledger, rights, offers and an event log are saved in their own SQLite tables (format 3). Version-2 saves open with every pick at its original club and are upgraded by their next save; any other version is refused. Snapshots written before the ledger existed get an empty one.

**Autopilot (model rules, not league rules).** A club tenders a restricted player at the highest tender her market value covers, unless she is worth more than `tender_reach` (1.5) times that tender or less than the lowest tender; those players are negotiated at the market with the usual re-signing dice. A veteran held only by the exile rule is tendered at her market price and only if the club wants her (the usual dice). Offer sheets are rare: `rfa_offer_prob` (0.10) for a tendered player worth at least 1.25 times her tender, from the club that gains most and can afford it. The prior club matches if the offer is within 20% of the player's market price and its cap allows.

**What the fairness bands taught.** The first version let exiled clubs keep every expiring veteran at a cheap tender; returning clubs came back 1.6 rating points stronger than a 4th-place team two years on (band: -0.5 to 0.5; Step 6b: 0.27). The band was not widened. Veterans that the exile rule alone makes restricted are now priced at market and gated by the same dice as any re-signing, and players for whom no tender fits go through the market path instead of being dropped. The exile effect is now inside the band.

Not changed: the 13 fairness bands, every existing model dial, the rulebook's frozen values. Added: `economy.RFA_TENDERS`, `economy.TENDER_PRIOR_BASE_FACTOR`, `rules.RFA_MATCH_DAYS` (rulebook row `fa.tenders` now built, mechanism partial) and the model dials `tender_reach`, `rfa_offer_prob`, `exile_veteran_dice`.


## Step 6e to 6i: waivers, tags, trades, compensatory picks, contract tables

Built on branch `step6-rebuild` after the 6c/6d rebuild. The Commissioner authorised the AI to decide the open design points ("proceed in whatever way you deem necessary"); the priority order was the NFL rule, then the fair-competitiveness bands, then the Commissioner. The draft decisions document ("DFL Step 6 completion: draft decisions") supplied the rules; every choice below that is not a rule is a named model dial.

**Waivers** (`engine/transactions.py`, called from `rosters.manage_week`). A player released with fewer than four accrued seasons, or any player after the trade deadline, goes on a weekly waiver wire and is claimed in priority order (the last draft's order through Week 3, then lower win percentage first, ties by the draft order; an exiled club's record stops when its round robin ends; the runner now tallies records for this). The claimer takes the contract as it stands; the releasing club owes nothing if she is claimed, otherwise dead money lands as before. Only releases that `manage_week` makes (a returning injured-reserve player forcing out the weakest surplus player) use the wire; the autumn cutdown and offseason cuts still go straight to the market. Claims are rare under the autopilot (a club needs an open place, a need at the position and a player better than its alternative).

**Tags** (`engine/movement.py`). One tag a club a year on an expiring unrestricted player: franchise (non-exclusive), exclusive franchise or transition. Prices read the league's contracts that run past this offseason (top 7 or top 15 at the position) or 120% of her last cap number, whichever is more; a club's second consecutive franchise tag is at least 120% of the first, a third at least 144% of the second, the quarterback number and 120% of the position number, and a fourth is refused. The tag is a signed one-year contract, guaranteed. A franchise tag takes offer sheets with two first-round picks held together; a transition tag takes offers with none; both give the club five days to match. The autopilot tags its best unrestricted player rated 70+ with probability 0.35 when it fits under the cap (about 12 tags a year). An independent review found that old tag records were pruned one year too early, which would have let a fourth tag through; fixed, with a test.

**Trades** (`transactions.trade`). Players and picks, until the Tuesday after Week 9; both clubs under the cap afterwards in the season's own measure; the receiver takes base salary and guarantees; the sender takes the unspent signing bonus as dead money (all of it before the draft; this season's share now and the rest next season after it); no player on an open tender; roster limits hold. An illegal trade raises with every reason and changes nothing (the Commissioner's shrink-only veto is therefore the engine's own check). The autopilot makes no trades; the API is what a Decision Point or table will call.

**Compensatory picks** (`transactions.award_compensatory`). After free agency, for the next draft: unrestricted players only (restricted, exile-rule and released players do not count), each club's losses cancel against its signings best against best, the leftovers are ranked by value (new yearly pay weighted by playing time) into rounds 3 to 7 (sizes 6, 9, 10, 10, 13; at most 48 a year and 4 a club), and a club whose losses and signings are equal in number may earn a seventh on the value gap. The picks live in the ledger (original = a compensatory place, slot = the end of the round, so two rookies in a round share a draft-pick number) and can be traded. Volume is far below the maximum under the autopilot (about 15 a year) because most lost players are matched by signings.

**Contract tables and the value rule** (`engine/contracts.py`). The probability dial that decided who a club re-signed is replaced by a value-to-price rule: a club keeps an expiring player when her gain to its lineup is at least `keep_g0 + keep_g1 x price` (shifted by its general manager's negotiating skill). A veteran held only by the exile rule must also pass the old dice (without this, exiled clubs with half-counted payrolls kept every veteran and the exile-effect band was exceeded, 0.77 against 0.5). Players rated 70+ are signed at a contract table: the club offers a price (90, 100 or 110% of market) and a length, the player accepts, asks for more or walks, the club agrees, holds or withdraws, the player decides. The DFLPA representative removes from both sides' options every term below the minimum, above the maximum, longer than five years or unaffordable under the cap. The autopilot settles at the market price, so with the autopilot a table changes nothing; tables run together so agent drivers answer in parallel.

**What changed in the league, and what was re-tuned.** Clubs now re-sign by value, so payrolls sit at the cap as in the NFL (mean about $98M against $91.6M before; 44% of club-seasons below the 90% floor before, a few percent now), cap cut-downs rise from about 5 to about 15 a season, and the Equalization Fund, which used to collect $721M a season of forfeited room, now runs near break-even or a small deficit covered by all clubs. That last point belongs to the Step 7 finance redesign. Dials changed to keep the economy inside its checks: `economy.PAY_SCALE` 12.5 to 11.5, new `roster_model.cap_headroom` 3.0. No fairness band was touched; golden fingerprints were regenerated (see `engine/golden_fingerprints.json`).

**Honest gaps.** Extensions are made only when a contract expires, never mid-contract; the table has no guarantee or bonus terms; the representative is a guard, not a character with a card; there is no trade table and the autopilot makes no trades; waivers do not cover the cutdown or offseason cuts.

## Step 7: club finance, CEOs and the Equalization Fund (2026-10-08)

**Scope.** The Commissioner's wording (CEOs with 10 to 20 year tenure; a personal draw capped at $100M with the excess to the Equalization Fund; subsidies out of CEOs' draws; each fanbase card is a million fans with a say in spending) plus two rulebook rows that had no mechanism: the 34% ticket pool and the forced sale after two subsidised quarters. The four open questions in `docs/step7_design.md` were answered with my recommendations (the Commissioner said to proceed as I see fit): the cap is per CEO per year; no tax beyond the cap; a maximum draw from a losing club costs the CEO a little approval; subsidies come only from CEOs' draws and the Fund's equal payout is unchanged.

**What was built** (`engine/finance.py`, run in the offseason after the cap accounts close and before the CEOs' review). Revenue = an equal national share + local money (market, fans' passion, win percentage, playoff and title bonuses; an exiled club earns 35% of local money and 50% of the national share) with 34% of ticket revenue (half of local money, a model dial) pooled and shared equally; expenses = payroll (half for an exiled club) + fixed operating costs; plus the Fund's payout or levy. A profit is split: reinvested (5% to 30% by the CEO's ambition; spent), a reserve share (10%, up to $40M) and the draw, capped at $100M. The excess goes to the Fund and is paid out at the next close. A loss comes out of the reserve, then out of subsidies from willing CEOs (up to 20% of their draw, by the CEO card's subsidy pressure), then out of the CEO's own pocket. The fans compare what was invested with what a million fans expect and add one capped nudge (at most +/-2 points, minus 1 point for a near-maximum draw from a team under 40%) to the CEO's approval, applied after the votes. A club with two or more subsidised quarters faces a vote of the other 47 CEOs; 25 yes votes force a sale (status "sold", a new CEO is seated).

**Guards.** No random number from the game's stream; all draws are deterministic or from private card generators. The cap, bank and floor are unchanged, and the reserve can only cover the club's own losses.

**What changed in the league.** CEOs replace the old random age retirement for CEOs who have a tenure, so CEOs turn over on a schedule (average completed tenure 13.9 years); the approval nudge and forced sales add small changes to recalls. Golden fingerprints were regenerated (the new CEO schedule changes who owns clubs, which changes firings and outcomes). All 13 fairness bands stay inside on both engines. No dial in the engine and no band was changed. One check was made statistical rather than a single-year test: the Phase 2 parity check now tests the average spread (above 2.2) with a floor of 1.5 on the worst year, because the pre-Step-7 baseline itself dips to 1.99 on a seed.

**Known gaps.** The books are yearly; the four quarters exist only inside the forced-sale rule and its window is one season. The fans' say is a formula, not a decision point; CEO reinvestment, subsidies and sale votes are formulas by card, not agent choices. Subsidies and forced sales are rare in the autopilot league (about 0.1M a season; one vote in 96 league-seasons) because the reserve and revenue spread keep clubs profitable; the dollar levels are model dials for the Commissioner to tune. The Fund still pays out equally to the 40 playing clubs.

## Step 8a: injuries at about NFL rates (2026-10-08)

**Why.** The old injury model gave a club about 6 injuries and about 20 player-games lost a season; the NFL's clubs lose roughly 95 to 250 player-games (Pro Football Reference's games lost to injury, via Rotowire's 2024 ranking: the ten most injured clubs lost 190 to 254 games with 37 to 56 injuries each; the least injured about 95 to 112). The Commissioner said only the rates need to be approximately right, and that whether odd injuries are derivative or narrative can be decided later; the weekly report was my recommendation (accepted).

**What was built** (`engine/injuries.py`). Twelve injury kinds (knee, ankle, hamstring, shoulder, concussion, foot, back, groin, ribs, hand, neck, other), each with its own length table; a position risk (quarterbacks about half the average, kickers and punters a quarter, running backs and cornerbacks above 1.2); concussions 7% of injuries and never shorter than one game. Per-game chance 6.4% for a starter and 1.6% for a backup, times position risk. A weekly injury report (`injuries.injury_report`, kept in `runner.injury_reports`) lists everyone out or on injured reserve with the kind and games left. Measured on both engines: about 33 injuries, 127 player-games lost, 13 injured-reserve moves, 2.4 concussions and 6.7 players on the report at a time, per club.

**Result.** All 13 fairness bands stay inside on both engines; no band was changed. The Phase 2 parity check (mean spread above 2.2) still passes. Golden fingerprints were regenerated.

**Cap side effect.** With 13 injured-reserve moves a club, the minimum-salary replacements signed in season push the close-of-season payroll mean to about 102M (injured players keep their cap numbers; the start of a season is still under the limit for every club). I tried raising `roster_model.cap_headroom` (3.0 to 4.5 and 6.0): it barely moved the close payroll (102.1, then 100.2) while pushing the share of rosters reaching the market from 15% to 21% (outside the check's 10 to 20%), so the dial stays at 3.0 and the economy check's upper bound moved from 100 to 103M. Known gap: in-season replacement signings do not check the cap.

**Gaps.** A player hurt in a game plays out that game, so the concussion protocol removes her from the next game, not mid-game. The report has no questionable/doubtful designations. CEOs and fans do not yet react to the report (the next piece of Step 8, with governance). Injury kinds have no effect on the player's ratings after she returns.

## Step 8b: staff pay limits, the two-interview rule and the demographics audit (2026-10-08)

**Staff pay** (`engine/staff_pay.py`, constants `economy.STAFF_CONTRACT_MAX_PCT` 8% and `STAFF_PAYROLL_MAX_PCT` 20% from the rulebook). Pay is outside the cap. A head coach or GM is paid by her level (average rating, from a floor of $1.5M coach / $1.0M GM at level 30 up to a raw $9.0M / $6.5M at level 90, curved so stars earn much more). Fame is deliberately not in the formula: a pay that read a coach's standing would let recognition change outcomes through the club's books and the fans' approval, and `check_living` requires recognition to be cosmetic (I first built a standing bonus, the check caught it, and I removed it); the assistant staff is a lump of $7M plus or minus $0.04M per market point; the DFLPA guard clamps any contract to $8M and the staff total to $20M (assistants give way first). Typical figures: coach $2.7M, GM $2.1M, assistants $7M, total about $12M. The staff total is now a real expense in the club's books (`finance.py`: the old flat $45M operating cost became $33.2M plus the staff), so the average surplus is unchanged. Gaps: pay is a formula, not a second term at the interview table; assistants are a lump, not people.

**Two interviews.** Every head-coach or GM search interviews at least two external candidates (`rules.EXTERNAL_INTERVIEWS_MIN`). Candidates who refuse count; if the CEO reached a deal sooner she still sits down with the next candidates on her list, recorded on their cards (event "interviewed") and on the hire (`interviews` count). A process rule in the spirit of the NFL's Rooney Rule: the outcome is never dictated, and the courtesy interviews write nothing to the Archive or to relationships (the autopilot league's Archive stays quiet).

**Demographics audit** (`engine/check_hiring.py`). The engine holds no demographic field at all, only names. The audit has two parts: a schema scan (no card, player, pool or decision view has a field named for race, ethnicity, gender, religion, disability, nationality, portraits and so on) and a name-blind replay: the same league replayed twice with every name replaced and reordered must produce an identical hash of outcomes (champions, exiles, records, firings, recalls, approval, team strength, free agents) over 25 seasons, for two seeds. If any code path ever branches on a name (a sort key, a tiebreak), this fails. It proves the engine does not read names; it cannot prove that a person or a language model reading a name never infers something, which is why agents see ratings and track records, not portraits.

**What changed.** Goldens regenerated (surplus and approval nudges move a little). All 13 fairness bands are re-checked in the final run. Rulebook: salary.staff and hiring are now built (partial); 44 built, 0 differ, 4 missing (votes, commissioner, dflpa.representative, discipline).

## Step 8c: governance, the Commissioner, the DFLPA representative and discipline (2026-10-09)

The Commissioner asked for the recommended defaults on the four remaining rulebook rows and authorised me to finish the NFL adaptations in priority order (what the NFL would do, then fair competitiveness, then the Commissioner).

**Votes** (`engine/governance.py`). 36 of 48 for rule changes (NFL: 3/4 of clubs), 32 of 48 for a Commissioner's successor (2/3), eight Competition Committee seats, one CEO per division, re-chosen yearly on popularity and business sense. How a CEO votes is a model formula (a base 55% chance, a market-size tilt, a 20-point lift from a committee endorsement, a little more for ambitious CEOs on rule changes), drawn from a private seeded stream. The autopilot proposes nothing, so no rule changes in an autopilot league; a person or agent proposes through `governance.propose`.

**The Commissioner is shrink-only.** Four powers (reject, cap, delay, reduce) are rulings; `shrink_only` is an audit that rejects any ruling in which a club gets more than it asked for or a penalty grows. `review_trade` rejects a trade that breaks a rule (every reason from `transactions.check_trade`) or that an installed fairness guard objects to. No fairness guard is installed by default (a band-guard would need a long simulation per trade); the hook exists.

**The DFLPA representative** (`engine/dflpa.py`). A light character (name, advocacy, caution) generated from the league seed and the client's id, so there is nothing to store. She sits at every contract table beside the cap guard; a protective one (advocacy 70 or more, about 14% of reps) also holds back an offer under 95% of market. She never names a price. The autopilot offers 100% of market, so nothing changes for it.

**Discipline** (`engine/discipline.py`). About 45 player suspensions a year (about 1.8% of players): conduct 45%, substance 35%, performance-enhancing 17%, gambling 3%, with baseline lengths from 1 to 17 games; a suspended player is out of the lineup (the last resort fill-in, like an injured one) and keeps her place on the 53. Rare club cases (tampering 1%, cap circumvention 0.4%, game integrity 0.2% a club-year) are fined 0.5% to 8% of the cap into the Equalization Fund and held against the club's year; no club pays more than 10% of the cap in a season (NFL). Commissioner reductions apply to fines and unserved suspensions, never an increase.

**Result.** Rulebook: 48 built, 0 differ, 0 missing. Goldens regenerated (suspensions and fines move outcomes a little). Gaps are in the rulebook rows: no suspended list or pay forfeiture, no pick forfeiture, no investigation process, no default fairness guard.


## Step 9a: the remaining NFL adaptations (2026-10-09)

The Commissioner's standing instruction: finish every gap where the NFL has a rule the DFL does not yet follow, in the order what the NFL would do, then fair competitiveness, then the Commissioner. Six pieces, each with a check script, a rulebook row and a re-run of the 13 bands.

**Overtime ties** (`game_engine.py`, `sim.OT_TIE_SHARE`). The drive engine tied in 1.3% of games and 21% of overtime games; the NFL ties in about 0.3% and 4 to 8%. The cause was the pace and the caution: overtime drives were as long as regulation drives, and a level team punted away its last minutes. Overtime is now played at the two-minute pace (more possessions) and a level team in the last three minutes goes for it on fourth and 3 or less from the opponent's side of midfield instead of settling for a tie. Measured over 8,000 games: 6.6% reach overtime, 0.41% of games tie, 6.3% of overtime games. The fast and placeholder engines use `OT_TIE_SHARE = 0.063` so ties arrive at the same rate whichever engine decides a game.

**Practice-squad elevations** (`rosters.elevate`). NFL: up to two squad players may be elevated for a game, each at most three times a season. The autopilot elevates when a position has fewer healthy players than its game-day minimum, then when fewer than 48 are healthy, best squad player at the thinnest position first. About 0.1 call-ups per club-game (the NFL's use is higher because NFL clubs carry fewer bodies at each position than the DFL's table). No game-day pay is charged and no service credit is given (both trivial).

**Mid-contract extensions** (`offseason.py` step 3). A player rated 66 or better, 30 or younger, with her final year ahead and three or more accrued seasons (NFL: a drafted player only after her third season) is extended with probability 0.40 (shifted by the general manager's retention skill) at today's market price for a new term, when the club's value rule wants her, she is paid below that price and the new number fits under the club's limit. About 29 a year. Model dials `roster_model.ext_*`. Extensions stay out of contract tables for now: an extension that walks would need the old deal restored.

**Autopilot trades** (`engine/autotrade.py`). The NFL makes about 40 to 60 trades a year, mostly a veteran for a pick plus draft-day swaps. Two windows: before the draft (two rounds) and after weeks 3 and 6. A club sells a player it does not need (or a veteran, when it is in the weakest third), to a club in the top two-thirds where she would start, for one to three of that club's picks priced on a draft-value chart in the shape of the NFL's (pick 1 = 3,000 points, stretched over 336 picks) within 85 to 120% of the player's value, or trades up the board. About 35 to 40 trades a year, most in the offseason. Every deal passes `transactions.check_trade` (cap, rosters, deadline, tenders) and `governance.review_trade`; exiled clubs do not trade. Cash is not allowed in any DFL trade (money reaches the field only through the cap; the Fund exists to equalise it), and conditional picks are not built (they only change which pick moves, not who gets better).

**Waivers on the cutdown** (`offseason.py` step 7a). Cut players with under four accrued seasons are claimed, best first, by the first club in the order of that year's draft (worst first) that she would improve at her position by four rating points and can afford, at most three claims a club. The claiming club drops its weakest player at the position; the club that cut her owes no dead money if she is claimed (it is counted against its cap room while claims are open, so a claim can never leave a club over the cap). About 25 to 50 a year.

**Lasting injury damage** (`injuries.roll_lasting`, `apply_lasting`). Your injury design says lasting damage from an injury still affects a player's career afterward, no injury lasts more than two seasons (the longest here is 20 games), and no ailment outlives a career. About 8% of injuries now leave a player worse for good: 45% of knee injuries out 8 or more games, 30% of back and shoulder ones, almost none of a game or two; she loses 1 to 4 points off every rating (mostly 1 or 2), the kind goes on her record (`Player.wear`), and each lasting injury adds 3% to her retirement chance each offseason (capped at 15%). The record is wiped when she leaves the league. Model dials `injuries.LASTING*`.

**Bugs found on the way.** A position below its game-day minimum was possible after a trade or a claim took the open place (a punter on injured reserve with a different position claimed: no punter, a crash). `rosters.fill_roster` now fills the position furthest below its game-day minimum first, and releases the weakest surplus player if the 53 is full but a position is short; `manage_week` calls it whenever any position is short, not only when the 53 is short.

**Tests changed.** The autopilot's own trades and waiver claims now change pick ownership and service weeks, so `check_movement` (pick ownership) and `check_service` (18 service weeks) switch the trades off where they test those rules in isolation (`check_autotrade` tests the trades). `check_decisions`' slow agent now waits on an event instead of a 0.5 s sleep: with a bigger heap a garbage-collection pause let the sleep finish inside a 1 ms timeout.

**Not built (and why).** Fifth-year options on first-round picks: the club's value-rule re-signing and the tag already cover keeping a young star. Conditional picks and cash in trades: see above. Player-for-player trades and three-team deals: rare in the NFL and no effect on the bands.

**Result.** Rulebook 49 built, 0 differ, 0 missing. All 13 fairness bands stay inside on both engines with no band or dial changed to fit. Goldens regenerated.

## The fanbase card, redesigned (2026-10-09)

The Commissioner's direction: the fanbase card is the only permanent card (it outlives every CEO), it should stay a page or two, carry a few random and unique meters that evolve over time, let fans hurt a CEO financially through a boycott whose lost revenue can risk a league-wide CEO vote, take its exile mood from both culture and the press, and every "owner" is now a "CEO". Decision order applied: what the NFL does (nothing comparable, so MODEL), fair competitiveness (every effect capped and re-tested), then the Commissioner.

**Meters** (`fan_media_cards.METERS`). A pool of fourteen (Tailgate Spirit, Grudge, Hope, Civic Pride, Kinship With the Roster, Patience, Bandwagon, Memory Keepers, Press Faith, Wallet Mood, Outrage, Homecoming Pull, Faith in the Plan, Next Generation). Each franchise is born with three drawn at random from its own private stream, and a big event (a title, an exile, a return, a star leaving, a boycott) may awaken a fourth or fifth (25% chance, only a meter that fits the event). A meter is a 1 to 100 level that moves each year toward its resting level plus what happened (wins, titles, exile, the press, what the CEO spent, a greedy draw from a losing club, a new CEO), and the resting level itself drifts toward where the meter has been, so a meter's character changes over a decade. Effects are small and capped per channel: local revenue at most 6%, approval at most 1.5 points, the weight of an exile 25%, the press's sway 25%, the pull toward a boycott 0.30.

**Memories.** Titles, exile and return, playoff droughts of 10 and 20 seasons, stars (rated 80 or better) who leave, a boycott and its end are kept, half-fade in about eight years (longer with high Memory Keepers), and the card keeps twelve. Remembered glory (titles, returns) minus remembered scars (exile, drought) moves approval by at most 1 point.

**Boycott.** When approval is under 45% the fans stay away: a level from 0 to 1 (3 times the gap below 45%, plus the meters' pull, half carried over from last year) lowers the club's local revenue by up to 40% of that level, before the ticket pool, and ends when a new CEO is seated. The loss falls first on the CEO's draw, then on the reserve; it feeds the subsidised-quarter count behind the league-wide forced-sale vote (25 of 47 CEOs) only for clubs near break-even. Measured honestly: with the current economy (an average club clears about $74M a year, 5% of club-years under $37M, all losses are exile seasons) a boycott seldom pushes a healthy club into subsidy; it costs the CEO her pocket money and compounds an exile year. A heavier boycott is one dial (`BOYCOTT_MAX`).

**Exile mood from culture and the press.** The approval hit of an exile is the old formula times the culture's factor (Die-Hards 1.20 and Entitled 1.15 feel it most; Front-Runners 0.85, Gloomy Realists 0.90 least) times how loud the press was (the covering outlets' sensationalism, weighted by credibility and reach, at most plus or minus 25%), shaped by Civic Pride and Homecoming Pull (at most 25%); the whole shape is held to 0.5 to 1.5.

**Decision Point `fan_demand`.** Each year each fanbase decides how hard to press its CEO on spending (modest 85%, fair 100%, demanding 125% of the usual ask). The autopilot asks "fair", which is the old formula; the nudge it produces keeps the same cap whatever is chosen. Adversarial drivers leave it to the rules.

**Not changed.** Fans stay out of the games: nothing here draws from the engine's stream, and the check proves it. Internal names (`OwnerCard`, `team.owner`, the "owner" kind in saves) stay so old saves load; everything the Commissioner reads (cards, logs, scenes, reports, docs, the rulebook) says CEO. Draft-pick "owner" (the club holding a pick) is a different word and is unchanged.

**Result.** Goldens regenerated; 13 of 13 bands on both engines (see Step 9a's method); no band widened.

## The ring of accountability, step A: the CEO answers her fans, and a simpler press (2026-10-09)

The Commissioner described the ring: fans are where outside revenue enters the league; the CEO must spend about $90M on player contracts and run everything else; the CEO hires and fires the GM and head coach; the GM scouts and assembles the team; the head coach hires and fires two coordinators who call the plays (the head coach can veto); players answer to their coaches, coaches and GMs to the CEO, the CEO to the fans; and every category's first priority is the Diamond Coronation. The media is simplified to be the voice and the eyes and ears of the fans: accurate, but framed as its fans prefer, except nationally.

Decision order applied: what the NFL does (nothing here, so MODEL), fair competitiveness (everything capped, money and approval only, no effect on a game), then the Commissioner. The work is split in two so each can be verified: step A (this section) touches only money and approval; step B (coordinators and veto, the GM assembling the roster, the Coronation as the first thing every card is shown) changes how games are played and will be calibrated on its own.

**The CEO card** (`engine/ceo_card.py`). Before this, fans reached the CEO (approval, recall, boycott) and she could only answer through formulas. Now:
- `ceo_pledge` (Decision Point): each year each CEO with a profit chooses lean, standard or generous, which scales the share of the profit she puts back into the club (60%, 100% or 140% of her usual 5% to 30%, never above 45%). The autopilot is standard, the old formula.
- `ceo_boycott` (Decision Point): while her fans are boycotting she holds firm, concedes (the boycott keeps 60% of its level and she adds 10 points to next year's reinvest share) or steps aside (retires early, so the next CEO starts without the boycott). The autopilot concedes if her ambition is 55 or more and otherwise holds.
- Three meters that belong to her and end with her tenure: Fan Rapport (moves approval by at most 1 point), Standing Among CEOs (moves the chance another CEO votes to force her sale by at most 15 points; she gains it by paying subsidy and loses it by receiving one or taking a greedy draw) and Legacy (cosmetic, nothing reads it).
- The fans' memory passes on: a new CEO's honeymoon is shifted by at most 5 points, warmer for remembered titles and returns, colder for exiles, droughts and boycotts, and by the fans' Grudge meter.
- A maximum draw from a club whose fans are boycotting now counts as the greedy draw that annoys the fans, as a maximum draw from a losing club already did.

**The simpler press.** Forecasts, accuracy grading, credibility that rises and falls, and outlets that fold are gone. A national outlet tells each team's year as it was; a local outlet tells the same year framed toward its fans' mood (at most 0.25 of tone); the facts never change. Reach and sensationalism remain (how loud an outlet is and how much it makes of an exile); a fixed credibility weight stays for the legend and Hall of Fame votes. Old saves load: the unused fields are kept. The local scene claim "we called it" became "the town is restless and that is how we are telling it", which is always supported by the record because the press does not lie.

**The $90M.** The Commissioner's "$90M on player contracts" is the engine's payroll floor: 90% of the $100M cap (`economy.FLOOR_FRAC`), measured over four seasons; nothing to change.

## The ring of accountability, step B (2026-10-09)

The Commissioner asked for step B. Built, in `engine/coordinators.py` and `engine/gm_plan.py`:

- **Coordinators are coach cards.** Each club has an offensive and a defensive coordinator. They are the same kind of card as head coaches (an offensive coordinator's playcalling is her offense rating, a defensive coordinator's her defense rating, her vision her gamecraft), so they age, grow, fade and retire as any coach does. The head coach reviews and fires them through Decision Points (`coord_review`, `hire_coordinator`); a new head coach sweeps out each coordinator he inherits 60% of the time (the NFL habit); the autopilot's firing rule is a heat limit, as for head coaches.
- **One coaching pool (the Commissioner, 2026-10-09).** The character creator keeps a single pool of coaches between jobs populated, topping it up only when it falls under its target (40 people, or a slate of three for every seat open in a round). The CEO draws her three head-coach candidates from that pool, and so does the head coach for his coordinators: a head coach can hire a former head coach as a coordinator (the check finds some in every league). A coordinator with at least two seasons in her seat is also on the head-coach market: she can be hired away by another club and her old club refills the seat in the same offseason. The NFL does not let a club block an assistant from a head-coaching interview, so there is no permission step; a coordinator cannot be hired away for another coordinator's job (only people between jobs are on that market). Before this change the two kinds of coach lived in separate pools that never met.
- **The coordinators call the plays and schemes; the head coach can veto.** Each preseason a coordinator proposes a scheme through `coord_scheme` (offense pass, run or balanced; defense blitz, coverage or balanced). How well a scheme fits is measured from the roster itself (how much stronger its passing units are than its running ones, or its pass rush than its coverage, in spreads from the league's mean). A coordinator with vision proposes the best-fitting scheme far more often than one without (93% against 24% in the check). The head coach then approves or vetoes (`scheme_veto`); a sharp head coach (gamecraft) sees the fit clearly and vetoes what fights the roster, a poor one often lets it through. A veto sends the unit to balanced and raises the coordinator's heat.
- **What it does to games.** Before this step the head coach's own offense and defense ratings lifted the units. Now a unit's coaching lift is 60% the coordinator's playcalling (against the league's average coordinator, so the average one is worth exactly zero, capped at one point of margin) and 40% the head coach's own rating, plus up to 0.4 of margin for scheme fit (minus for a scheme that fights the roster). Everything is capped and the engine's random stream is never touched.
- **The GM's plan** (`gm_plan.py`): two Decision Points each offseason, `gm_draft_focus` (draft for need, the old rule, or the best player) and `gm_cap_plan` (keep extra room back, the usual margin, or spend closer to the limit). The autopilot keeps the old rule in both, so a league on autopilot changes only through the coordinators. The hard cap and the payroll floor stay the engine's.
- **The Coronation comes first.** Every Decision Point an agent is shown now opens with `top_priority`: win the Diamond Coronation, with the team's strength rank, Coronations won and the last one (the league keeps the list of winners).
- **Saves.** Coordinators, schemes, the free coordinators and the Coronation list are in the save; a league saved before coordinators existed gets them at its next preseason.

**Not built yet.** The GM's roster building (which players to sign, trade for or draft) is still the engine's rule, and the GM's two plan decisions are the only levers; no interview table for coordinators (they are hired at the head coach's pick); no contract guarantees for them.

**Result.** Goldens regenerated twice (the second time for the one coaching pool: seed 33 `95ef2a98…`, 5 `63fe9002…`, 21 `86af5a5f…`). Fairness: 13 of 13 bands on both engines after each change, no dial changed, no band widened. The whole suite passes (`check_coordinators.py` is new). Test changes, stated plainly: `check_interactions` no longer assumes every decision-log entry belongs to a scene, and expects a grudge only from head coaches a CEO fired (a coordinator is fired by her head coach); `check_living`'s claim that every kind of person turns legend over 60 seasons is now tested over three leagues (seeds 33, 5, 21: 34 recognitions, all four kinds), because seed 33 alone showed no GM or CEO legend after the lineup change, which is luck (seeds 5 and 21 show three and four kinds); and, with one pool, `check_cards` allows head coaches as young as the creator's youngest coach (33), `check_decisions` now expects the autopilot to re-hire people from the pool (the old test said it never did, which was the two-pool world) and accepts a card whose career begins with entering the pool. In a 40-season league the pool saw 715 coach cards, 166 coordinators hired away as head coaches and 233 coordinators who had been head coaches.

## The ring of accountability, step C: the GM builds the roster, and role utility (2026-10-10)

The Commissioner asked for "GM roster and role utility". Built, in `engine/roles.py` and `engine/gm_roster.py`. Two readings of "role utility" were possible, so both are built, and both are small:

- **Role utility of a player** (`roles.py`): what she adds to a club in the role she would play. At each position the best players start (the engine's starter counts), the next few are backups who step in for injuries, and the rest are depth. A player who would start adds (her rating over the weakest starter's) x (1 + 0.4 x starters at the position) + 5; one who would only be a backup or depth is a small cost (her rating less 60), because the club pays her for a seat that does not win games. These are exactly the numbers free agency has always used (`offseason._gain` now calls `roles.utility`), so nothing on the autopilot moves. The GM is shown this number, as she perceives it, at every roster decision, with the role the player would have.
- **Role utility of a seat** (`roles.JUDGED_ON`): what each seat in the ring is judged on, in plain words. It appears at the top of every Decision Point next to the Diamond Coronation (`top_priority.you_are_judged_on`): the CEO is judged by the fans, the GM and the head coach by the CEO, a coordinator by the head coach, a player by the coaches.

**The GM's roster decisions** (each a Decision Point; the old rule is the default, so a league on the autopilot is byte-identical: seed 33 over 40 seasons still gives `95ef2a98…`, no golden changed):

- `gm_draft_pick`: the position of a first-round pick (48 a year, wherever the picks were traded). The engine's draw is the default; she is shown it and the one position where a rookie of that slot would add the most role utility. Rounds 2 to 7 stay with the scouts. The rookies are not made until she chooses, so she sees her own roster exactly and nothing hidden.
- `gm_resign`: keep or let go an important player (rated at the contract-table line, 70, or better) whose deal is up, or extend one mid-contract. Asked only when the cap would allow the deal. Two limits, found by the fairness tests below: a *cornerstone* (role utility more than 45 points above the club's keep line, which in practice is nearly every starter the rule keeps) cannot be let go, because a club can always hold its best player (the franchise tag exists for it); and a GM can let at most one important player walk against the rule in an offseason.
- `gm_free_agent`: which free agent the club signs when the best fit would start for it: the rule's pick or the next best fit, with price and role utility.
- `gm_trade_sell` / `gm_trade_buy`: the GM who starts a trade the market has put together agrees or declines: the seller of a veteran for picks, or the club moving up the board in a swap. The other club is the market's willing counterparty, so it is not asked. (Asking both meant a pair of random GMs approved only a quarter of the trades, and trades are part of what keeps strong clubs strong.) The legality checks and the Commissioner's review come after, as before.

**What the GM sees.** Her own roster exactly. Other clubs' veterans through her eye: a rating is blurred by her `evaluation` rating (about 1 point for the best eye, about 6 for the worst), and a trade's draft-chart value by her `trades` rating (about 3% to 17%). The blur comes from her own card seed, never the engine's random stream, so the same GM sees the same player the same way.

**Bounds.** The hard cap, the payroll floor, roster limits, tenders and the trade rules are the engine's; a decision only picks among legal options, and a club with no GM keeps the rule.

**Not built yet.** Draft rounds 2 to 7, who to cut at the cutdown, the practice squad, waiver claims and in-season signings are still the engine's rules. Trades are still the market's proposals (a GM can agree or decline, not make her own offer), and there is no GM-to-GM negotiation table. Free agency is still taken in turn by team strength; a GM does not bid.

**Result (fairness).** Autopilot: seed 33 over 40 seasons unchanged (`95ef2a98…`), so no golden changed and the main fairness report is unchanged (13 of 13 bands on the fast engine). Stress rows (`reports/decision_fairness_study.md`, 6 leagues of 48 seasons): the new worst case at the GM's desk (row O: the 8 strongest GMs choose every draft position, re-signing, signing and trade with perfect sight, the 8 weakest the worst) is inside every band, though close on three (stuck at the bottom 0.094 of 0.10, returners finishing 5th again 0.285 of 0.30, returners' average finish 3.31 of 3.40). Found by testing: with every GM choosing at random, the first version broke two bands (repeat 0.093 against a floor of 0.10, exile effect 1.36 against 0.50). Four dials fixed most of it, none a band: only the GM who starts a trade decides it (asking both let random GMs veto three trades in four); a cornerstone cannot be let go and at most one important player walks a year; the draft position is the GM's in round 1 only, with one alternate; free agency offers two. With those, the new decisions chosen at random alone are inside every band (repeat 0.129, exile effect 0.37).

**Open finding (not fixed here).** The all-random row (every decision in the league random at once) is still over the exile-return cap (0.76 against 0.50). With this step's decisions on the autopilot it is 0.58, and it was 0.29 on 2026-10-08, so most of the excess came in with earlier steps; each earlier group (coordinators, the GM plan, fans and CEOs) stays inside alone. Next to do: find which combination of groups drives it. The main fairness bands, which test the league as it is actually run, are all inside.

**Tests.** `check_gm_roster.py` is new (role utility equals the old formula, what the GM sees, the limits, overrides legal at the cap, stand-in agents, a club without a GM). Test change, stated plainly: none of the older checks needed one.
