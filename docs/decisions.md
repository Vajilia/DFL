# DFL decision log

The authority for every rule is the living doc **"DFL Rulebook: The Synthesis"** (https://claude.ai/code/artifact/0e15335e-d40e-4e7a-8d2a-e9eccedb24ea), frozen as v1 on 2026-10-06. It decides each rule by one test, in order: the Commissioner's skeleton, then the NFL rule, then the NFL rule adapted to 48 teams / 18 games / exile, then the fair-competitiveness bands. Its machine-readable form is `engine/rulebook.py`; `engine/check_rulebook.py` fails when the code and a rulebook row disagree, and writes `reports/rulebook_status.md` (every rule, its basis, and whether the code matches).

There is no "assumed" tag any more. Every rule has one of four bases (skeleton, nfl, adapted, fair); `engine/rules.py` carries the same labels, and simulation stand-ins that are not league rules are labelled `model`. The old decisions are archived in `docs/decisions_pre_rebase.md` (reference only).

The Commissioner delegated the open rulebook decisions to the AI on 2026-10-06 ("you have the authority"), to be decided by the NFL rule first and then by fair competitiveness. The alternatives for each are listed at the end of the Rulebook, so any row can be reopened.

To change a rule: change the number in `engine/rules.py`, update its row in `engine/rulebook.py`, and run `engine/check_rulebook.py`.

## Step 6a: earned-service foundation (2026-10-07)

Current decision order, authorized by the Commissioner: first ask what the NFL would likely do and why, adapted to DFL's schedule and structure; then test fair competitiveness; refer unresolved design choices to the Commissioner-elect. Existing DFL skeleton rules remain fixed.

The NFL distinguishes accrued seasons (six regular-season games on qualifying full-pay status, for free agency) from credited seasons (three, for minimum pay). Source: https://operations.nfl.com/calendar-events/nfl-free-agency/types-of-free-agents (checked 2026-10-07). Keeping the separate clocks prevents unsigned or practice-squad time from manufacturing free-agent rights or salary service.

`service.py` records qualifying games for the 53-player active/inactive roster and injured reserve, awards service once per season, and classifies expiring contracts as ERFA/RFA/UFA. Ambassador round-robin games count under the existing DFL rulebook. Byes, the Ambassador Bowl and playoffs do not count toward this regular-season service model. Practice-squad players earn service only after permanent promotion to the roster. Mid-season releases retain service earned before release; it is cumulative across teams, with a week counted only once. Counters and partial-year weeks persist through the existing player-body JSON save.

Historical limitation: founding roster players and old saves have no game-by-game service history, so their prior calendar-years estimate is retained once. Founding practice-squad players, drafted rookies and undrafted rookies start at zero. Future seasons are earned. NFI/PUP, suspensions, holdouts and injury settlements are not yet modelled and are not silently counted.

This is deliberately an accounting checkpoint: minimum salaries and practice-squad eligibility still use the old calendar-years field, and the re-signing policy is unchanged. The classifier does not enforce retention rights. Tenders, offer sheets, matching rights, tags, waivers and trade/pick ownership must be integrated before Step 6 can be called complete. Thus `fa.classes` has matching constants but a **partial** mechanism. The NFL itself makes ERFA rights conditional on offering a minimum one-year tender; classification alone cannot restrict a player.

## Step 6b: service controls eligibility and minimum base pay

Credited seasons now control market-price floors, founding contracts, offseason re-signings and signings, street replacements, promotions and continuing-contract minimum base pay. Accrued seasons now control the existing DFL practice-squad eligibility rule. Calendar years still govern age/career history; unsigned and practice-squad years no longer create salary or eligibility service. Newly generated street players have zero documented earned service rather than inferring it from age. Existing founding and old-save estimates remain unchanged.

The NFL minimum is base salary, not base plus bonus. A new contract caps its bonus allocation at the amount left after the credited-service base floor. This preserves the DFL rookie scale as an initial cap number; for a $0.30M late-round rookie, $0.29M is base and only $0.01M can be bonus proration. Continuing deals are raised when the earned minimum rises, without rewriting existing bonus proration or guaranteed dollars. A four-year low rookie deal therefore need not remain a flat cap number. The usual offseason cap/cutdown checks run after these increases. No pay-scale or fairness dial was changed.

Sources: NFL Football Operations, types of free agents and credited seasons (https://operations.nfl.com/calendar-events/nfl-free-agency/types-of-free-agents); contract language and minimum base salary (https://operations.nfl.com/calendar-events/nfl-free-agency/contract-language); NFL/NFLPA CBA Article 26 (https://nflpaweb.blob.core.windows.net/website/PDFs/CBA/March-15-2020-NFL-NFLPA-Collective-Bargaining-Agreement-Final-Executed-Copy.pdf).

Existing DFL simplification: the frozen practice-squad rule permits players with no more than two accrued seasons without the NFL's combined ten-player category limit and allows at most six veterans. Actual NFL eligibility has additional active-game categories and a combined quota. This increment corrects the service clock within the frozen DFL allocation; it does not claim exact NFL practice-squad implementation. Elevations remain unmodeled.

Step 6 is still incomplete: ERFA/RFA tenders, offer sheets, matching and compensation, tags, waivers, trades and draft-pick ownership/trading/compensatory picks remain. Re-signing still uses a probability policy rather than a contract table.

Validation complete: all twenty check scripts passed on the corrected Step 6b code, including six service and four salary/eligibility test cases. Both-engine fairness studies passed all thirteen bands, and the four-league cap study was refreshed. Interim pre-CBA results are superseded. Three forty-season hashes were regenerated for explained rule changes; no model dial or fairness band changed. See reports/verification_step6b.md and its JSON receipt for exact scope and log hashes.

Primary-source correction: NFL/NFLPA CBA Article 26 Section 2 explicitly excludes IR from salary-credited service, unlike accrued service for free agency. The NFL summary page did not state that exclusion. Step 6b therefore persists separate credited-service weeks; active/inactive roster games qualify for both clocks, IR games qualify only for accrued service. The same DFL Ambassador-round-robin inclusion applies to both clocks. Practice-squad, bye and postseason time earns neither. New boundary tests cover two roster games plus four IR games (accrued season only) and three roster games plus three IR games (both seasons).

Legacy migration limitation: old saves lack the roster/IR distinction. Closed historical service counters remain one-time estimates. For an old partial-year save missing credited-service weeks, its existing qualifying weeks are copied once as an estimate; subsequent seasons reset both lists and use the correct distinction. No past IR history is invented or silently reconstructed.


Regression-test corrections: the initial full run on corrected Step 6b engine code passed 18 scripts and failed two legacy assertions. The coach-centering test inspected a cached pre-turnover reference; it now explicitly refreshes the current staff reference at the next-season boundary. The media test measured a noisy final-year credibility snapshot (correlation 0.03); it now measures mean scored forecasts over careers with the same 0.05 threshold and separately checks that a better forecast earns more credibility against the same result and starting score. These are test-only changes; simulation code, model dials and fairness bands are unchanged. Both corrected scripts passed on rerun; the media career-score correlation is reported in the check log.
