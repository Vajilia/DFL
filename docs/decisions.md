# DFL rule decisions

Change a number in `engine/rules.py`, then update this file.
Every rule is tagged **confirmed** (Jeph stated it) or **assumed** (the AI filled a gap). Assumed rules are working defaults only. They are never treated as settled.

## Confirmed

| Decision | Setting | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| League name | Diamond Football League | Jeph, 2026-10-03 |
| Structure | 48 teams, 2 conferences, 8 divisions, each division 5 active teams + 1 exile slot | rules text |
| Schedule | 18 games over 19 weeks (1 bye): 4 non-conference same-tier, 4+4 division, 6 tier-based | rules text |
| Tier opponents | 1s play 1s and 2s; 2s play 1s and 3s; 3s play 2s and 4s; 4s play 3s and 5s; 5s play 4s and 5s | rules text |
| Tier vs seed | "Tier" (1-5) for scheduling, "seed" (1-7) for playoffs | rules text |
| Exile | Finish 5th in the division; one season; returns as Tier 5; Ambassador Season (7-game round-robin + Ambassador Bowl) | rules text |
| Playoff field | 4 division winners + 3 wild cards per conference (7 seeds); seed 1 has a bye | Jeph, 2026-10-03 |
| Draft bands | Picks 1-8 exiled lottery, 9-34 active non-playoff, 35-48 playoff teams | rules text |
| Lottery size | 8 teams | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| Recall cycle | One division league-wide per year, so every owner is voted on once every 8 years | Jeph, 2026-10-04 (replaces an earlier wrong "4-year" change) |
| Recall also triggers on exile | Yes | rules text |
| What exile is | The league treats exile as help for a distressed team, not punishment: relief plus play in exotic locations around the world while it rebuilds. Media and fans may see it differently. | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| Tiebreakers | All tie-breaking mirrors the NFL, at least for now (division, wild card and seeding, draft order). Procedures in `engine/tiebreak.py`, checked against nfl.com on 2026-10-04 | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| Lottery format | 8 teams, the ones that just finished 5th. The best record of the eight holds 1 ball, the next best 2, and so on up to 8 balls for the worst record (36 balls). Four balls are drawn for picks 1-4; the other four teams pick 5-8 in order of record (worst first), so the worst team falls no lower than 5th. Chance of pick 1, worst to best: 22.2 / 19.4 / 16.7 / 13.9 / 11.1 / 8.3 / 5.6 / 2.8 percent; chance of a top-4 pick: 75 / 71 / 65 / 58 / 50 / 39 / 28 / 15 percent | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| The final | Named the **Diamond Coronation**. The champion is presented with a **Diamond Tiara** instead of a trophy. "Super Bowl" is the old name from the rules text and may still be used in conversation | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| Priority | Fairness comes before narrative. The narrative (fan and media story) is derived from the fanbase and media, not designed in | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| Exile and the two drafts | Lottery pick as a new exile, then a block pick at the end of the 9-34 band (picks 27-34, ordered by Ambassador Season record) when back from exile. Accepted as the working design | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| Test standard | Every test asks one question: does the trend fall outside the expected / accepted range of "fair competitiveness"? | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| How exile shows up in the story | Not set by rules. It emerges from how the local and national media and fanbase react (a long-suffering Cleveland-style market versus a cutthroat "he's not Montana or Young" market give very different stories). That belongs to the character, media and fan layer built later | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| Character cards, first slice | Build players first, then coaches (owners, GMs, media and fanbases later). Cards are generated by code from the league seed so the same seed gives the same people, with a writing layer for backstories and scenes added later. Coach cards may change results only by a small capped amount, re-tested against the fairness bands | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| How much help | Modest. A returning team should have the potential to compete for 3rd place in its division, sometimes succeeding, sometimes not. Promotes fair competitiveness. | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |

| Aging and retirement | Players improve until their mid-twenties, hold, then decline; kickers, punters and quarterbacks last longer. Retirement chance rises from about 31 |

| Numeric bands for "fair competitiveness" | Thirteen measured trends with low/high limits (win-percentage spread 0.13-0.19, repeat champion at most 12% of seasons, title concentration no worse than pure luck among the 14 playoff teams (an average of 4.7 titles for the top franchise in any 20 years, and no single league above 7), exile never worth more than half a rating point, a returner averages 2.8-3.4 in its division, and so on). Defined in `rules.FAIR_COMPETITION_BANDS`, measured by `engine/fairness.py`, reported in `reports/fairness_report.md`. The AI's first proposal; Jeph may change any limit |
| Archetypes are souls | A player's positive and negative archetype are fixed for life, read from the rating profile she is born with. Her development is bent to stay true to them: each offseason her ratings move back 35% of the way toward the profile her soul gives her (her overall rating is not changed by this) | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| Personality is an expression of the soul | Her core trait comes from her archetype's family of four traits, her fixed temperament, and her perception of her own ratings. Perception lags the truth (declining veterans overrate themselves, rising youngsters undersell themselves) plus a lasting ego bias. As perception moves her personality can shift, but only to another trait her soul allows. Interpretation by the AI: "perception and personality can change, bounded by the archetype; the archetype itself does not" | Jeph, 2026-10-04 (wording), AI (mechanics) |
| Coaches enhance their players, within the bands | Every coach has a development rating: a 100 adds 0.4 rating points a year to each young player (half that to players over 27), a 1 takes it away. This is on top of the team lift (a 100 offense or defense coach is worth +1.0 point of margin). Both are sized from the sweep in `reports/coach_fairness_study.md`: a development effect of 1.0 breaks the title-concentration bands, 0.6 is inside but close, 0.4 is inside with room. A team lift of 1.5 was also inside on the fast engine but left too little room on the drive engine (best team on paper winning the title 21% against a 25% limit), so 1.0 was kept | Jeph, 2026-10-04 (direction), AI (sizes) |
| Legends are emergent, never designated | There are no legend coaches or players by birth, no legend rate, no cap on how many there can be, and no limit of any kind on greatness (Jeph: "i want that to be an emergent property that the media can take notice of"). A person's honors (titles, All-League, Player of the Year, Coach of the Year, Executive of the Year) build a career **esteem**; each of the 52 media outlets reads a person's esteem with its own noise (a local outlet reads its own team's people kindly); the credibility-weighted share of outlets calling someone a legend decides it, and when they split she is **contested**. Once a legend, she keeps the title with a little extra room, so it does not flicker. What I set is the *standard* (the esteem bar, `recognition.LEGEND_BAR`, a placeholder dial per kind of person), never a count. It was set so recognition is rare enough to mean something while still happening more often than in the NFL (about 8 coaches, 4 GMs, 5 owners and 14 players in a 100-season league, roughly 0.8 coach legends a decade; see reports/emergence_study.md). Legend status is mostly reputation and story: on the field, effects stay capped at the same small sizes for everyone | Jeph, 2026-10-04 (principle), AI (standard and mechanics) |
| Player cards | Name, hometown and path depend only on the player id and never change. Pressure thresholds (exile, contract, spotlight, loyalty) are stored but do nothing yet. Relationships and decision log start empty. Retired players keep their cards for the Archive |
| Coach cards | One head coach per team, six ratings (offense, defense, development, gamecraft, discipline, motivation; 50 is average). Offense, defense and development act on results; the other three are stored for later. Coaches stay until they retire (3% a year before 60, rising after, nobody past 74) and the team hires a new one. Owners firing coaches comes with the owner cards |
| Owner accountability, as built | One owner per team. Each year the owners of the rotation division are voted on, as are the owners of newly exiled teams and anyone whose fan approval is under 40%. A vote is 1,000,000 fans by simple majority; 5 replacement candidates are generated and the fans pick one (the more popular candidate tends to win). No one ever owns a team twice (the check script verifies this over 40 seasons). Rules text, confirmed | Jeph (rules text) |
| What drives fan approval | Now the fanbase card (see the row below). The first version was a placeholder formula in `engine/staff_cards.py` and is kept behind a switch (`fans=False`) so the fairness study can compare against it. Either way a vote tips to "recall" when approval is under 40% (fans who neither approve nor vote to recall are counted at 10%) | AI placeholder |
| Hiring and firing | Owners never touch a game directly. An owner whose team under-delivers builds up "heat" on her coach and GM (it decays 40% a year; exile adds nothing on its own, because an early version that did made the exile effect too strong). The coach is fired when heat passes a line set by the owner's patience (roughly 7 coaches and 5 GMs a year across the 48 teams); a legend needs twice the heat. A newly seated owner sometimes sweeps out the staff on arrival (more likely for hands-on owners, never a legend). Fired coaches and GMs leave the league; the team hires a new one, drawn like any other | AI placeholder |
| General managers | One per team. Two small levers: a 100-rated scout lifts her team's rookie by 1.5 rating points a year (a 1 costs the same); a 100-rated negotiator cuts her team's yearly contract expiries by 30% (a 1 raises them by 30%). Sized by `reports/staff_fairness_study.md`. Three other ratings (evaluation, trades, cap sense) are stored for later | AI placeholder, tested against the bands |
| The Archive, first entries | `league.archive` logs recalls (with approval, vote share, candidates and the winner), owner retirements, coach and GM hirings, firings and retirements, as plain facts. The party-claims narrative format in the rules text comes with the media and fan layer | AI |
| Fanbase cards | One per team; it belongs to the franchise and outlives every owner. Six cultures (Die-Hards, Front-Runners, Long-Suffering, Entitled, Gloomy Realists, Party Crowd) give the personality, wants and fears; five ratings (loyalty, expectations, passion, volatility, media trust) bend how approval reacts to a season: loyal fans move slowly, demanding fans want more wins, passionate fans take an exile harder, moody fans swing, trusting fans listen to the press. Expectations drift with results but never more than 15 points from where the fanbase was born (your rule that personalities move within bounds, applied to fans). The culture also decides which owner trait fans look for among the 5 recall candidates. Approval and Fan Capital are stored on the card; the owner's approval is a mirror | AI placeholder, built on the rules text |
| Fan Capital | The rules text's "recall immunity" currency. A slow average of recent success (0 to 1). It only ever buffers a recall vote (at most 15 points of vote share, usually a few), never makes one more likely. A first version that could also hurt an owner raised recalls by about a quarter, so it was made protective only | AI placeholder, built on the rules text |
| Media cards | Four national outlets and one local beat outlet per team (52 cards), each with a voice (Hype Machine, Watchdog, Contrarian, Homer, Statistician, Gossip), five ratings and Credibility, the rules text's "narrative control" currency. Each season every outlet forecasts its teams from their strength and is graded on the miss; credibility moves toward the score. An outlet that stays under 18 credibility for two seasons folds and a new one takes its place (the Archive logs it). Outlets read a team's year through their own bias (local outlets lean homer, sensational outlets stretch it). The press as a whole can move an owner's approval by at most 3 points a year (more in a big market, to a trusting fanbase). The media wants clicks and fears irrelevance, per the rules text, and irrelevance is what folds an outlet | AI placeholder, built on the rules text |
| Media and fans never touch a game | They change one number, approval. Approval decides recalls and a recall can change who fires the coach or GM. The check script verifies they draw nothing from the game's random stream and that a first season is identical with the cards on or off. Re-tested against the fairness bands (reports/fan_media_fairness_study.md) | AI |
| Interaction system, first slice | Built in this order, as you chose: the three Required interactions that already happened as plain log lines (firing, recall vote, exile determination) become real scenes first; Random, Crisis and Economic interactions come later, Economic last because the engine has no money yet. A scene has parties, each with a claim, an evidence section and an outcome, in the rules text's "Party A claims / Party B claims / Evidence supports" form | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| Scenes never change an outcome | The decisions (who is fired, recalled or exiled) are still made by the same rules as before. A scene explains a decision, writes each decider's decision log and moves relationships (-100 to +100, stored on the cards). Relationships are not used by any rule yet. Verified by a check: 40 seasons play out identically with scenes on or off, down to every win percentage and the engine's random stream. Any later field effect would be small, capped and re-tested against the fairness bands | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| How a claim is judged | Each party's claim carries a frame (for example "the roster was thin", "the team under-delivered", "the press did it", "we called it"). The engine's own numbers decide whether the frame holds: record against what the roster predicts, roster strength against the league, last year's record, Fan Capital, the press's effect on approval, a local outlet's forecast miss. Evidence defaults to truth, per the rules text. Coaches who blame the roster overstate it by about half a standard deviation on average, so some excuses hold and some do not (about half held in a 40-season run) | AI placeholder, built on the rules text |
| Rulings on a firing | Each firing is ruled a sweep (a new owner clearing the staff), fair (the record backs the owner and the roster does not excuse it), harsh (the record was poor but the roster explains it) or unfounded. In a 40-season run: about 30% sweeps, 57% fair, 11% harsh, 1% unfounded. The firing rule itself is unchanged, so a harsh firing is a real finding about the placeholder rule (it fires on results alone, not on results against the roster), left as it is for you to decide | AI placeholder |
| Relationship amounts | A fired coach or GM feels it more the more loyalty matters to her (loyalty threshold 1 to 100 scales the blow from half to one and a half times). A recall breaks the owner-fans relationship regardless of past goodwill; surviving a vote warms it by how decisively the owner survived. An exile makes the owner cold toward whoever she blames. All amounts are named dials in `engine/interactions.py` | AI placeholder |
| Scene text | Code-generated from the cards' own personalities and the engine's numbers, so every claim is checkable. A prose layer can be added later over the same facts | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| The guiding principle: road, car, driver | Anything that can be acted on by an agent is not deterministic. The road, its guardrails and its traffic controls are code (schedule, game physics, tiebreakers, lottery, exile rule, caps, the evidence in every scene). The car is a card's capability (its ratings). The driver is the agent who chooses what to do with it. Agents choose actions, never outcomes, and whatever they choose the league stays inside the fair-competitiveness bands | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| Decision Points | Every consequential choice goes through one door (`engine/decisions.py`): the engine builds the decision (who decides, what she perceives, the legal options), a driver picks one option id, a guard validates it and falls back to the autopilot on anything invalid, and the choice is applied and logged. Agents choose from options the engine enumerates; free text exists only as a stored, short reason that is never obeyed. A free-form proposal (for example a trade offer) waits for the money system | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| The autopilot | The rules the engine had before agents (heat-based firing, first-candidate hiring) are kept as the default driver. It is used when no agent answers in time, for tests and for the baseline in the fairness studies, so a season can always be completed. Proof: it reproduces the pre-agent league exactly (hash-identical history on three seeds of 40 seasons, `engine/golden_fingerprints.json`) | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| The choice log is the history | Every choice is logged (who, what was on offer, what was chosen, by which driver, whether the guard accepted it, the reason). The same seed and the same choice log give the same league, so a history stays auditable even though agents are not reproducible. "Same seed, same league" now holds for the autopilot and for replays, not for live agents | AI, from the principle |
| What an agent sees | Its own card, and what it perceives, not the truth. Candidates' ratings are shown as the owner perceives them: the truth plus her blind spots, which are smaller for a shrewd operator (business 100: about 3 points off) than for a money pit (about 11 off). So an agent can be wrong, and the evidence in a scene is what corrects it | AI placeholder |
| First pilot: the owner's keep, fire or hire choice | Each season each owner chooses keep both, fire the coach, fire the GM or fire both, and each hire is one of three candidates (the autopilot takes the first, as the old rule did). Coach and GM retirements now go through the same door; the owner's choice among recall candidates is still the fans' formula and is next in line | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| Fairness is tested against the worst legal move | Because a real agent may play anything legal, the bands are tested against drivers that see the true ratings of every candidate and push staff quality to the legal limit (every owner churns and hires the best; only the strongest teams do; the strongest hire the best and the weakest the worst; everyone hunts for the most famous coach, everyone recycles the same people between jobs), as well as random legal play. See reports/decision_fairness_study.md | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| Hall of Fame | Retired coaches, GMs, owners and players are eligible after waiting 3 years (on the ballot for 6) if their esteem is at least 55% of the legend bar. Every active outlet (weighted by credibility) and every owner votes yes if their noisy reading of the person's esteem is above 80% of the bar; she goes in with 75% of the vote. No cap and no quota: some years induct several, most none | Jeph, 2026-10-04 ("Media plus a league Hall of Fame"), AI (mechanics) |
| Living cards | Coaches, GMs and owners are persistent people like players. Each has a **soul** fixed at birth (her best and worst rating name her archetypes, and the shape of her ratings is kept), a hidden **potential**, an ego, a temperament, and a self-image that lags the truth. Over a career her ratings grow toward her potential (coaches and GMs until 52), hold, then fade from 62, always pulled back toward the shape of her soul; an owner's ratings stay near where they began and her popularity follows her fans. Her personality can shift, but only to one of the four traits her soul allows. She retires on a curve (coaches from 60, GMs from 62, owners from 65), and everyone keeps a card, a trajectory and an Archive entry forever. New people start around 50 with the same spread as before | Jeph, 2026-10-04 (souls fixed for life), AI (mechanics) |
| Effects are measured against the league's average | A coach's or GM's effect is her rating against the average of the coaches (GMs) on the job right now, so the average person on the job has exactly zero effect however careers move the ratings, and the league cannot inflate in talent or drift. Effects keep the same caps (coach lift 1.0 point, development 0.4 a year, GM scouting 1.5 points, retention 30%) | AI |
| Reputation only touches owners' choices | Esteem buys a coach rope with her owner (the firing threshold rises with esteem, doubling at the legend bar, capped at 2.5x), famous coaches are not swept out by a new owner, and a famous candidate gets a halo in how an owner *sees* her ratings (up to about 3 points at the legend bar; her true ratings are unchanged). Nothing in recognition touches a game: a test plays 25 seasons with rope and halo set to zero and shows the league is identical with honors, esteem and the Hall on or off | AI |
| People between jobs | A fired coach or GM is not retired: she joins the people between jobs, ages, changes, may retire or drift out after four idle years, and is offered to owners again as one of the three hire candidates (not by the team that fired her for 3 years). Candidates an owner passes over join the same pool. The autopilot still hires the first candidate, as the old rule did, so the pool matters only when agents choose from it | AI |
| Retirements go through Decision Points | A retiring coach or GM is now replaced by her owner through the same keep/fire/hire machinery (retiring GMs are new: before, GMs never retired). The autopilot's pick is the same card the old rule hired | Jeph, 2026-10-04 ("convert the rest"), AI |
| Agents work in batches | Decisions that do not depend on each other are asked together (`decide_many`): all the retirements, then all 48 owners' yearly reviews, then every hire that follows, so a dozen agents can answer at once and a league season costs about three rounds of answers, not one per decision. The order of the log, the ids and the results are exactly what asking one at a time gives (proved with a stand-in agent). A slow or failing agent costs only her own decision, which falls to the autopilot; with agents in all seats the autopilot league is bit-for-bit unchanged (golden fingerprints) | Jeph ("a dozen or so agents" are enough because they are punch cards for the cards), AI (mechanics) |
| Cards are the memory, agents are the workers | A decision's payload is built from the deciding card: a bounded view (ratings, pressure, reputation, honors, career summary, her last five decisions and her own notes to herself). The agent may write a short plain-text note (240 characters; the card keeps the last eight) that goes through the guard onto her own card and is shown back to her next time. Notes never change an outcome and one card's notes are never shown to another. Any agent can be plugged into any seat, because the seat's whole history is on the card | Jeph, 2026-10-05, AI (limits) |
| A stand-in agent for rehearsal | `engine/agents.py` is a small rulebook that reads only the public view a real agent would see (it cannot reach the engine's state), so owners with different personalities fire at different rates and favour different candidates. It rehearses the machinery and the fairness bands, not a real model's judgment. There is not yet a path that calls a real model from inside the engine | AI |
| Persistence: the cards are the save | A league is saved to one SQLite file (`engine/store.py`). Everything about a person lives on her own card (soul, ratings, trajectory, honors, career, her notes to herself, every decision she has made; a player's record also carries her body), one self-contained JSON record per person, fanbase and outlet, plus a small league record (who sits where, counters, flags, random stream) and the Archive, choice log and Hall of Fame. Loading rebuilds the league from those alone, and a league saved at season 20 and played to 40 equals the uninterrupted one, card for card (the order of each card's ratings included). A whole-league snapshot per year is optional (`snapshot=True`) for reopening an earlier year and is tied to the code version that wrote it; the cards are not. A save refuses a league that has state it does not know about, two different cards with one id, and a file in a different layout. One coach's or GM's card can be exported and placed in another league's pool of people between jobs (`export_card`, `import_card`); players and owners cannot change leagues | Jeph, 2026-10-04 ("persistence") and 2026-10-05 ("store what the card does on the card itself"), AI (mechanics) |
| Golden fingerprints regenerated | The autopilot fingerprints in `engine/golden_fingerprints.json` were regenerated on 2026-10-05 because the living cards legitimately change outcomes (coaches, GMs and owners grow and retire; there are no designated legends). The old fingerprints belonged to commit 7c0e0dc |
| Commissioner as a backstop (decided, not yet built) | A Commissioner watches the live band statistics and may shrink how much a choice moves things if a band nears its edge. It never overrules a choice, and every intervention is logged in the Archive. Built after the pilot, as the next step | Jeph, 2026-10-04 |
| Meeting spaces are tables for the few important negotiations (decided, not yet built) | Only the moments where each party must have her own independent say get a table: contract negotiations, trade negotiations and job interviews. Team meetings, owner lunches and coach-player relationships stay generalized (the cards and the existing scenes drive storylines). A table has the parties, each with her own card and her own perceived view of the others, a bounded number of turns from engine-enumerated moves, and a deal only if both accept something the engine's limits allow | Jeph, 2026-10-05 |
| The job interview, the first live table | After an owner chooses a candidate, the two sit at a table (`engine/tables.py`, `engine/interviews.py`). The owner offers 0 to 3 guaranteed seasons (the owner cannot fire her in her first N yearly reviews; the engine removes the firing options while it lasts, whoever owns the team by then). The candidate accepts, asks for more, or walks away; if she asked, the owner agrees, holds or withdraws, and if she held, the candidate accepts or walks. A candidate who walks is out and the owner chooses again among the rest, each with her own interview; if all three refuse, the league office fills the seat by the old rule (first candidate, no guarantee), so a seat is never empty. The candidate sees public facts and her own noisy read of the owner (never the owner's card); the owner sees the candidate as she perceives her. The autopilot offers nothing and accepts, so the autopilot league is exactly the old league (golden fingerprints unchanged). Only terms are negotiable; pay will be a second term once the cap exists. Placeholder: the cap of three seasons | Jeph (tables for job interviews, contracts and trades), AI (mechanics: guaranteed seasons as the first term) |
| Tables run side by side | Every table is a short chain of Decision Points (so it is in the choice log and replays exactly), and the 48 owners' tables run in lockstep, each round asking every open table's next turn together, so a dozen agents answer in parallel; inside one table the turns are in order. A turn that gets no valid answer falls to the autopilot's move, so a table always finishes | AI |
| Salary cap (decided, not yet built) | The DFL salary cap is $100 million and never inflates (the Diamond States of North America have no inflation). Unused cap space can be carried over and banked, but $125 million is the most a team may go into a season with (so at most $25M banked on top of the $100M); anything above that is forfeited to the league for redistribution, and the pool funds the exiled teams' cap relief (the 50% absorption already in the rules). A payroll floor of about 90% of the cap keeps teams from hoarding | Jeph, 2026-10-05 ("uber-tubular" to the AI's proposals for the forfeited pool and the floor) |
| The DFLPA representative (decided in outline) | Every contract meeting has an agent representing the player as her DFLPA representative: each character acts for herself but has someone looking out for her best interests, who may tell her she is asking for too much (and may negotiate herself into a second career) or that she is not being offered enough. The same or a separate Front Office representative may check that neither side breaks the rules. Open: whether these are one role or two, and whether they are persistent cards | Jeph, 2026-10-05 |
| Card word lists | Names, hometowns, backgrounds and personality types are placeholder lists in `engine/card_pools.py`, chosen by the AI. Names are unique across the league's history |

## Placeholder model (not rules)

How ratings turn into scores, how the draft changes team strength and how strength drifts between seasons are stand-ins so the league can run. The exile-related dials (pick-1 value 1.0, cap relief 0.5) were calibrated on 2026-10-04 so a returning team averages about a 3.2 division finish on the Phase 1 scalar model, in line with the confirmed exile intent. They live in `engine/placeholder_model.py` and must not be read as design decisions.

On the roster model (Phase 2) the same target was re-calibrated: the dials are in `engine/roster_model.py` and `engine/players.py`. A returning team now averages a 3.0 to 3.1 division finish (a league-average team is 3.0), with about 19% finishing 1st and about 22% falling to 5th again. See `reports/phase2_report.md`. Free-agency priority for returners was tried and dropped because it pushed the average to about 2.7.
