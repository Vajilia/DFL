# Whatever the agents choose: fairness under the worst legal play

The principle: the road has guardrails and traffic controls, the character card is the driver, and what the driver does with the car always stays inside the fair-competitiveness bands. The first owner choice to go through a Decision Point is keep, fire or hire for the coach and the GM (the hire is one of three candidates). The autopilot row reproduces the league as it was before agents. The other rows replace the owners' choices with random play and with adversaries that see the TRUE ratings of every candidate (no real agent can) and play the legal limit. Everything they do is a legal option; the guard would reject anything else.

## What this shows

Every row is inside every band: random legal play, nobody ever being fired, and four adversaries that see the true ratings of every candidate and push staff quality to the legal limit, league-wide and in the most unequal split (strongest teams hiring the best, weakest the worst). Here are the rows side by side (8 leagues of 48 seasons each, fast engine):

| Row | Typical title concentration (limit 4.0) | Worst league's titles in 20 seasons (limit 7) | Best-team title odds (band .08 to .25) | Exile effect (band -0.5 to 0.5) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A. Autopilot | 3.00 | 4 | 0.216 | 0.22 |
| B. Random legal | 3.12 | 4 | 0.194 | 0.30 |
| C. Nobody fired | 3.62 | 6 | 0.206 | 0.31 |
| D. Everyone churns, best hire | 3.62 | 4 | 0.219 | 0.19 |
| E. Strongest teams only | 3.50 | 4 | 0.194 | 0.37 |
| F. Strongest best, weakest worst | 3.50 | 5 | 0.209 | 0.23 |
| G. Everyone hunts a legend | 3.75 | 4 | 0.213 | 0.23 |

The reason is structural: a coach moves a team by at most about 1 point of margin on each side of the ball and the choice is one of three candidates, so even perfect hiring by every owner is a small, league-wide effect, and the exile, draft and tier machinery (the traffic controls) keep absorbing it.

## What the testing found, and the guardrail it produced

The bands held, but the legend-hunting adversary exposed something the bands do not measure. With no cap, every owner hiring a legend whenever one is on offer put about 19 of the 48 teams under a legend coach at once (the autopilot averages about 2.5 and reaches 8 at most in the 8 leagues measured). That contradicts the intent that a Walsh or a Lombardi come along every now and then. So the guard now has a **legend cap of 8 on the field at once**: with the cap reached, no legend is offered on an owner's list (a legend candidate is replaced by an ordinary one). With the cap, the same hunt gives about 7 on the field. Natural arrivals (a coach retiring and being replaced, or the league's first hires) are not capped, so one or two over the cap can still appear. The cap does not touch the autopilot on the golden seeds. The number 8 is a placeholder dial (`LEGEND_CAP` in `engine/staff_cards.py`); a lower number makes legends rarer.

## Limits of this evidence

These adversaries cover one decision (keep, fire, hire) and are chosen by me; a different adversary could find a different edge, so the study is a floor, not a proof. Eight leagues is a small sample for the tighter bands: the worst league's title count reached 6 (limit 7) when nobody is ever fired, which is worth watching. The adversaries see the truth, which no real agent can, so a real agent should do worse than these rows, not better.
