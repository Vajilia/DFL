# Payroll and the cap

Step 6e to 6i study: clubs now re-sign by a value-to-price rule instead of dice (contract tables, tags, waivers, compensatory picks, trades are in). Dials changed: economy.PAY_SCALE 12.5 to 11.5 and the new roster_model.cap_headroom 3.0. Before this step the same study read: mean payroll 91.6M, 44.1% of team-seasons below the floor, Fund surplus 370M a season, 5.3 cap cuts a season.

4 leagues x 48 seasons, fast engine, autopilot, first 8 seasons dropped. Every number is the AI's placeholder pay scale (economy.py); the rules ($100M cap that never inflates, $125M most a team may go into a season with, 90% floor over four seasons, exiled teams' payroll counted at half) are the Commissioner's.

## Where payrolls sit

Mean payroll 98.4M of the $100M cap; the middle 90% of team-seasons run 82.9M to 112.4M. 15.8% of team-seasons end below the 90% floor and 4.7% end at their limit. The five best-paid players take 28% of a team's payroll on average.

## Banked room

A team carries on average 14.7M of banked room into a season (most possible 25M); 40% of team-seasons begin with the full 25M and 3% with none.

## Dead money

A team carries on average 9.3M of dead money (bonus and guaranteed pay of players it let go); 4.5 cuts a season use one of the two post-draft designations that split the hit across two seasons.

## The Equalization Fund

Each season the Fund takes in 439M of forfeited room and pays out the league's half of exiled teams' payroll, 387M. In a year it falls short the 48 teams cover it equally (an average levy of 7M a season); above its 100M reserve the surplus goes equally to the teams that played (an average of 56M a season). Floor top-ups paid to players average 16.9M a season across the league.

## What the cap costs teams

Each season the league re-signs 1130 expiring players, loses 210.0 it could not afford to keep, and teams that spent banked room cut 16.4 contracts to get back under the cap.
